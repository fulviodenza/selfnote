Selfnote privacy policy

Last updated 10 October 2026. In one line: the developer of Selfnote collects nothing about you, and the only place your notes are ever sent is the server you connect the app to.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Selfnote clients: the iPhone, iPad and Android app, the web client that ships with every Selfnote server, and the desktop app for macOS, Linux and Windows. They are three builds of the same program and behave the same way. The separate Selfnote eReader has its own policy.

Selfnote is self-hosted software. There is no Selfnote cloud, no account with the developer, and no server in the middle. The app connects to one Selfnote server, the one you type in on first launch, and to nothing else.

What the app stores on your device

  • the address of the Selfnote server you connected,
  • an access token and a refresh token for your account on that server, kept in the app's private storage on mobile and in the browser's local storage on web and desktop,
  • a cache of every note you have opened on that device, so it opens instantly and keeps working offline (SQLite on mobile, IndexedDB on web and desktop),
  • interface preferences: which notes are open as tabs, which folders of the page tree are collapsed, and your light or dark theme choice.

Your password is not stored. You type it to sign in, the server answers with the two tokens above, and the app keeps only those. Nothing is written outside the app's own storage, and nothing is shared with other apps on the device.

What the app sends, and where

Before a server is configured, the app makes exactly one kind of request: when you type an address and tap connect, it asks that address whether a Selfnote server is running there. Nothing else is contacted, ever, by the app itself.

Once connected, everything the app sends goes to that server and only there:

  • your email address and password, to sign in or create an account on it,
  • the notes you write, as live edits over an encrypted connection, so they sync to your other devices and to anyone you share the workspace with,
  • files you attach to a note,
  • tasks, labels, links between notes, share links you create, and the calendar feed you turn on,
  • if that server has AI enabled, the text of the note or selection you ask about, your prompt, and the writing sample you optionally save for "Rewrite in my voice".

Who can see what you send is decided by the server, not by the app. In a shared workspace, other members can read the notes in it and see your name next to your cursor while you both edit a page. A share link makes that one note readable by anyone who has the link, until you revoke it. A calendar feed URL does the same for your tasks.

AI

AI features are off until whoever runs the server turns them on, and the app shows them only when the server says a provider is available. When you use them, the note text leaves your device for your server, which forwards it to the provider the operator chose: a Claude command line tool running on the server itself, the Anthropic API, or an Ollama model on the operator's own hardware. With the first and third option, your notes never leave that machine. With the Anthropic API, the text of the request goes to Anthropic under their terms. The AI guide explains how each is configured.

AI never writes into a note on its own. Every AI edit, whether it comes from the in-app assistant or from Claude connected over MCP, is staged as a proposal that you review as a before and after diff and accept or reject.

If the server is not yours

Many people run Selfnote for a household or a small team, so you may be using a server someone else operates. That person has the same access to your notes that any server operator has to the data stored on their machine: the database holds your notes, your email address, and a hash of your password. What they do with it is governed by your arrangement with them, not by this policy. The developer of Selfnote has no access to any instance.

What the app never does

No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting, no device identifiers, no push notification service, and no third-party SDKs that contact anyone. The app makes no request to the developer's servers because the developer runs none. Nothing is sold, because nothing is collected.

Children

The developer collects no data from anyone, including children. Whether children may have an account on a given server is up to the person running it.

Removing your data

On the phone, Log out in Settings removes the tokens and closes your tabs. Delete all data on this phone, also in Settings, removes everything the device holds: tokens, server address, the cached notes, and your preferences. Deleting the app does the same. On the web, clearing the site's data in your browser removes the tokens and the note cache. The desktop app keeps its cache in the system web view and does not yet offer a one-tap wipe; this is tracked in issue #28.

None of this touches the notes on the server, because they are on your server. Trash a note in the app to remove it from the workspace, and empty the trash to delete it. To remove your account entirely, ask the person who runs the server, or drop the database if that is you.

This website

This site is static pages served by nginx with no analytics or cookies. It loads two typefaces from Google Fonts, which means Google's servers see the request for the font files. Nothing else is loaded from a third party.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears here with a new date. Selfnote is open source, so every claim above can be checked rather than trusted: the mobile app is at apps/mobile, everything it stores is in src/api.ts, src/settings.ts and src/persistence/sqlite.ts, and the AI provider logic is in server/api/src/ai.rs.

Contact

Questions about this policy: open an issue. For help using the app, see the support page.
