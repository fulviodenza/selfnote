What people do with it

A workspace you host changes what you're willing to put in it. Some shapes Selfnote is built for:

Two people, one household

The shared life file

Trip plans, renovation quotes, the kid's school calendar, the caponata recipe. Both of you edit the same page at the same time — one of you on a plane. Promote "book the ferry" to a task with a due date and it shows up in your phone's calendar through the iCal feed.

Because it's your server, the file with passport scans attached isn't sitting in a startup's S3 bucket.

One person, many machines

The homelab logbook

Every service you run, how you installed it, and what broke last time — written where it can't be lost with the vendor. Link notes to notes and the 3D graph becomes a map of your infrastructure. The MCP server closes the loop: after Claude helps you debug something at 1am, say "save this to my notes" and the fix is filed before you forget it.

A researcher or writer

The thinking environment

Import your Obsidian vault, keep the backlinks, and gain real-time sync across laptop, desktop, and phone — offline on all of them. AI assist runs against Ollama on your own GPU, so drafts and sources never leave the building. Version history means you can always get back the paragraph you deleted at midnight.

A small team

The wiki that isn't a subscription

Meeting notes with live cursors, a page tree for the handbook, share links for the odd external doc — with view counts so you know when to kill them. Roles cover owner, admin, editor, and viewer. It runs on the Kubernetes you already have, backed up by CloudNativePG's point-in-time recovery, and costs whatever your cluster costs.

Anyone with a Claude subscription

The memory Claude writes to

With the MCP connector, any Claude — CLI, Desktop, or claude.ai — can file summaries into your instance and edit living documents in place. A "Feature ideas" note that Claude appends to over weeks, merging like a human collaborator, is a genuinely new kind of notebook.
