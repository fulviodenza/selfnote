Your agenda from a chat

Tasks in Selfnote are notes with dates, and the MCP server exposes them. Capture, ask what is overdue, and tidy up in plain sentences from any Claude.

Tasks in most notes apps are checkboxes that know nothing. In Selfnote a task is a note, or a block inside one, with a due date, a status and a priority, and the workspace has an agenda across all of them. Because the MCP server exposes that agenda, Claude can read it and change it from any conversation. Here is what that looks like day to day.

Ask what matters

"What is overdue?" Claude calls list_tasks with a status filter and a due window ending now, and answers with the titles, how late each is, and links that open them. "What is due this week on the renovation?" adds a parent note, so only tasks under that project page come back. "Anything tagged urgent I have not started?" filters by label and status. None of this needs you to open the app; the app is where you go to do the work, not to find out what it is.

Capture without switching

Half of task management is the moment of capture, and that moment is usually in the middle of something else. In a chat about planning a trip you say "make a task to book the ferry, due Friday, under the Sicily note". Claude creates a page task in the right place with the right date and tells you where it landed. Because tasks feed the workspace calendar, it appears on your phone's calendar through the iCal feed the next time the calendar refreshes.

Checklist items work too. "Turn the third item of my packing list into a task due Thursday" has Claude read the note with block ids, find the item, and promote that single block. The checklist stays where it was; it has simply gained a due date.

Keep it tidy

Tidying is where most systems die, because it is boring. With the MCP it is a sentence. "Mark the ferry booking done." "Move everything under Inbox that mentions the bathroom to the Renovation note." "Label the last five notes I made as homelab." Claude uses manage_task, organize_note and manage_labels, and each of those applies immediately because each is a single gesture to undo in the app. Moving, labelling and renaming never go through the proposal review, by design; body edits do.

Weekly review, assisted

A review is a conversation, and this one has a well informed partner. Ask Claude to list what you finished this week and what slipped. Ask it to draft the next week's plan from the overdue items and the ones due soon. Then say "save this review under my Weekly notes" and the plan is filed as a page, linked to the tasks it mentions. The following week, Claude reads last week's review first. The loop closes without a template.

Where the boundaries are

  • Claude cannot permanently delete anything. Trash is the furthest it can go, and trash is reversible.
  • Claude cannot change a note's body without review. Appending a line to the review page produces a proposal you accept; a task's due date changes at once. The difference is whether a mistake would be hidden.
  • Claude sees what your account sees. The token is yours, scoped to your workspaces, enforced by the server on each call.
  • Everything is on your server. The agenda, the notes, the proposals. The only thing that leaves is whatever your chosen model provider needs to answer, and that can be nothing.

Why this is better than a to-do app with AI bolted on

Task apps that add AI add a model that knows your tasks. A notes workspace with tasks in it and an MCP door gives the model your tasks and the pages they came from, the notes those link to, and the conversations that produced them. "Why did I decide to postpone the deck?" is answerable because the decision is a paragraph in a note the task lives in. Context is the product, and in this setup the context is yours.
