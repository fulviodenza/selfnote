AI summaries you can trust

The weak point of AI note taking is the moment output becomes record. How Selfnote separates drafting from applying, reviews remote edits, and records who wrote what.

The weak point of AI note taking is not the model. It is the moment the model's output becomes part of your record without anyone checking. A summary that quietly drops a caveat, a rewrite that changes a number, an "action items" list with one item invented: each becomes a fact in your notes the next time you read them. Trust has to be designed in, and this is how Selfnote does it.

Separate producing from applying

In the Assist panel and the note actions, the AI produces text that streams into a result area. It does not touch the page. You read it, then choose: insert at the cursor, replace the selection, copy, or retry. The output is a draft until you say otherwise. This sounds obvious and most AI editors skip it in the name of friction.

Make remote edits visible

When Claude edits a note over MCP you are usually not looking at the note; you are in a chat somewhere else. So those edits cannot apply directly. The server stages each one as a proposal: the change itself, computed against the note's exact current state, plus a Markdown rendering of the page before and after. The editor shows a button to review the pending edits. You open the diff, in the web, desktop or mobile app, and accept or reject. If someone edited the note while the proposal waited, the server recomputes the change against the new state, and marks the proposal superseded rather than forcing it if that is no longer possible.

The rule underneath is simple: a change to the body of a note is reviewed, because that is where an error hides. A change to its metadata, like a label or a parent, applies at once, because it is one gesture to undo and the review would be theatre.

Ground the model in the right text

Hallucination is mostly a context problem. The Assist chat is grounded in the note you are editing, and when that is not enough you add specific notes as extra context: the ones the current page links to, or the ones you just visited, chosen from a picker rather than guessed by a retrieval system. The model answers from what you gave it, and you know exactly what you gave it. A server side cap on context size keeps the request honest about what fit.

Keep a voice sample instead of a style prompt

"Rewrite in my voice" is only trustworthy if "my voice" means something concrete. Selfnote keeps a sample of your own writing, up to a few thousand characters, saved once in Settings and sent with each rewrite request. The result sounds like you because it was shown you, not because it was told to be "casual but professional". Clear the sample and the action falls back to a plain rewrite.

Record who wrote what

Every proposal keeps its origin, its before and after text, and who accepted or rejected it and when, so you can always see which edits came from a model and what they changed. Version history saves restore points automatically and on demand, and a restore is a new forward edit rather than a rollback, which means the record of the bad edit survives next to the fix. You can always answer "when did this sentence change, and who changed it".

Choose the model, and know where it runs

Trust in output starts with trust in the pipeline. The person running the server picks exactly one provider: a Claude command line tool on the server, the Anthropic API, or a local Ollama model, and the client displays which one is answering. If the answer should never leave the building, it does not have to.

Say no by default

AI is off on a fresh Selfnote server. There is no AI button until a provider is configured, and no background indexing of your notes to make a future button faster. When you turn it on you know what you turned on, because the switch is one environment variable and the code it enables is a file you can read.

The result

None of this makes a model more accurate. It makes the system around the model honest: the AI drafts, you decide; remote edits wait for review; context is explicit; authorship is recorded; and the whole pipeline runs where you can see it. That is the difference between AI that writes in your notes and AI that writes your notes.
