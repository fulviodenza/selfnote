Your notes are the context window now

Whoever holds your notebook decides what every assistant knows about you. The case for making that person you.

There is a quiet shift in what a notebook is for. It used to be a place you wrote things so you could read them later. It is becoming the place a model reads so that it can know you. Whoever holds that notebook decides what every assistant you use knows about your life.

Memory is the product

Models themselves forget everything between conversations. What makes an assistant feel like it knows you is the text it is handed at the start of each chat: the "context". Every AI company is racing to own that layer, because owning the context means owning the relationship. The chat app wants to be your memory. The notes app wants to be your memory. The browser, the email client and the operating system want to be your memory.

Notes have a structural advantage in that race. They are already written by you, in your words, about the things you actually care about, and they are already organised. A good notes workspace is the highest quality context a model can get, and it needs no scraping of your inbox to produce.

So the question is who you want holding it

If your notes live with a vendor, that vendor's model is the one with the deep access, and every other assistant gets whatever crumbs the vendor's integration allows. That is a commercial decision made for you. If your notes live on your server, you decide which model gets to read them, how much, and through which door.

Selfnote is built around that second shape. The server is yours. AI is a provider you plug in, and the MCP server is a door with a lock on it: a personal access token you mint, scoped to your account, revocable from the Connections screen the moment you change your mind.

What it feels like when the memory is yours

A few things stop being science fiction once your notes are reachable by the model you already pay for, on your terms.

  • You never finish a conversation empty handed. You and Claude spend forty minutes debugging a Kubernetes networking problem. You say "save this to my homelab notes". Claude writes a clean summary, files it as a sub-page under the right note, and hands you the link. Next time the problem recurs, the fix is where you will look for it.
  • Notes become living documents. A "Feature ideas" page that Claude appends to across weeks of unrelated chats. A reading list that grows as you mention books. Each addition arrives as a proposal with a diff that you accept in the app, so the page grows without ever being rewritten behind your back.
  • The model can answer from what you know. Ask "what did I decide about the database migration in March" and Claude searches your workspace, reads the note, and answers with a link to it. Your notes are the source, not a vendor's summary of them.
  • The agenda is a sentence away. "What is overdue on the renovation project?" is a question Claude can answer because tasks are notes with dates, and the MCP exposes them.

The guardrail that makes it safe

Giving a model write access to the record of your life is only sensible if it cannot quietly change the record. In Selfnote, every AI write to a note's body is a proposal: the server stores the suggested change next to a before and after rendering, and nothing is applied until you tap accept. Metadata changes that are trivially reversible, such as moving a note or adding a label, apply at once. The distinction is deliberate and it is explained in how living documents work.

Combine that with version history, where every restore is itself a new edit and nothing is ever destroyed, and you have a memory that a model can contribute to without being able to erase.

Own the context

The companies building assistants will tell you that the model is the thing. For most of what you actually want from an assistant, the context is the thing, and the context is your notes. Keep them where you can see the server, pick the model you trust, and open exactly one door.
