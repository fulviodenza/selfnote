Selfnote eReader

A reader for iPad that treats your highlights and your handwriting as yours. Read EPUB and PDF, mark what matters, and work through the exercises on blank pages inside the book itself, with Apple Pencil.

See what it does Read the support page

Books stay on your device · no account · sending highlights to Selfnote is optional

A shelf, a page, and room to think.

The shelf shows what you are reading and how today is going. A book opens on the page you left. When a page asks you to work something out, you add a page of your own right next to it.

What it does.

All of it works offline. Highlights bound for your Selfnote are queued on the device and sent on a later try.

EPUB and PDF

Open any DRM-free EPUB or PDF from Files, iCloud Drive, or anywhere else the document picker reaches. The app keeps its own copy, so the book still opens if you move the original.

Each book reopens on the page you left.

Highlight, and keep it

Select a passage and tap Save highlight. It works the same in an EPUB and a PDF, and your highlights live on the device with the book.

If you run Selfnote, connect your instance and choose a page for each book. From then on the book sends its highlights to that page, and they become notes in your own workspace, next to everything else you write. Connecting is optional; reading and highlighting never depend on it.

Work the exercises in the book

Add a blank page and write on it with Apple Pencil: after any page of a PDF, or at the end of a chapter in an EPUB. It is real PencilKit ink, with the same tool picker you know from the rest of iPad. Working pages sit between the book's own pages, and they never change the book's length or your progress through it.

Copy area lifts a region of any page, a figure or a problem, onto a working page as an image, so you can mark it up right where you are solving it.

Zoom

Pinch to magnify a PDF page. In an EPUB, a pinch changes the text size instead, and each book remembers its own.

Reading goals

Set minutes per day and books per year. The daily number is the time you actually spent reading; the yearly one counts the books you mark finished.

Made for iPad. It fits on iPhone too.

The large screen is where a page and your notes sit comfortably together. The same app runs on iPhone, with the same shelf and the same reader.

There is no account and no server of ours. Until you connect your own Selfnote, the app itself makes no network requests, and after that it talks only to the address you typed. The privacy policy has the details.

Coming to the App Store.

The eReader is on its way to the App Store. Until it is there, the support page answers the common questions, and the code is open on GitHub for anyone who would rather read it.
